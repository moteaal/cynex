Brushed DC Servo Motors

Brushed DC servo motors provide reliable and controllable motion for industrial machinery, positioning mechanisms, and electromechanical equipment. They offer relatively simple drive requirements and can be suitable for applications requiring adjustable speed, controlled torque, and responsive movement. Cynex Systems can help select appropriate motor ratings and configurations according to the application's electrical, mechanical, and operational requirements, while considering maintenance needs and overall cost.
Brushless DC Servo Motors (BLDC)

Brushless DC servo motors use electronic commutation instead of mechanical brushes, reducing brush-related maintenance and making them suitable for many demanding motion control applications. They can provide high efficiency, rapid response, and reliable operation when paired with a compatible drive and feedback system. Cynex Systems can evaluate brushless motor options for industrial automation, specialized machinery, and mechatronic projects, taking into account required speed, torque, operating conditions, and control compatibility.
Geared DC Servo Motors

Geared DC servo motors combine a motor with a gearbox to provide suitable output speed and torque for applications requiring controlled movement under significant mechanical loads. Different gearbox types and ratios can be considered according to the application's space limitations, positioning requirements, and mechanical characteristics. Cynex Systems can help determine suitable motor and gearbox combinations and, where required, develop customized mounting arrangements, shafts, couplings, and mechanical interfaces for integration into industrial equipment.
Coreless DC Servo Motors

Coreless DC motors feature a specialized rotor construction that can provide low inertia, smooth operation, and fast acceleration and deceleration. These characteristics make them useful for precision instruments, compact positioning mechanisms, testing equipment, and specialized mechatronic devices. Cynex Systems can assess whether a coreless motor is appropriate for a particular application by considering response requirements, torque, speed, thermal limits, available space, and budget, helping identify a practical solution without unnecessary cost or complexity.
DC Servo Motors with Encoders and Feedback Systems

DC servo motors equipped with encoders or other feedback devices can support accurate position and speed control when integrated with a suitable controller and drive. Depending on the selected feedback technology, these systems can provide motion information for repeatable positioning, controlled movement, and monitoring of operating performance. Cynex Systems can assist in evaluating motor, encoder, drive, and controller compatibility and developing suitable electrical interfaces for industrial machines, automated equipment, and custom-built control systems.
Customized DC Servo Motor Systems

When standard products do not fully meet an application's requirements, Cynex Systems can evaluate customized servo motor solutions based on the project's mechanical, electrical, and operational needs. Depending on technical feasibility and project scope, this may include selecting or adapting motor configurations, integrating gearboxes and feedback devices, designing mounting components and mechanical assemblies, and developing compatible electrical circuits and control interfaces. Our goal is to recommend a technically suitable and cost-effective solution that meets the required performance, installation constraints, reliability expectations, and budget of each industrial or mechatronic project.
Our Approach to Your Project

Cynex Systems follows an application-focused approach to identifying suitable products and engineering solutions.

Step 1 — Understand the requirement: Review the machine, load, motion profile, available space, power supply, and operating environment.

Step 2 — Define the specifications: Establish the required torque, speed, positioning performance, duty cycle, and control functions.

Step 3 — Compare suitable options: Evaluate commercially available motors and related components, considering technical compatibility and total cost.

Step 4 — Develop the proposed solution: Where necessary, investigate customized mechanical arrangements, electrical interfaces, drive selection, or integrated motion control assemblies.

Step 5 — Review feasibility and implementation: Confirm the design assumptions, component availability, project scope, testing requirements, and estimated cost before proceeding.

This process helps create a clear technical basis for selecting or developing a solution that meets the practical needs of the customer.
